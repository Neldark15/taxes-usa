# Virginia: Form 760 and State Rules

Virginia is the initial focus state because of the high concentration of Salvadoran diaspora in **Northern Virginia** (Arlington, Alexandria, Annandale, Falls Church, Woodbridge, Manassas). Understanding its specific rules is critical because **Virginia is not like federal**: it has its own agency, its own forms, and — most importantly — **its own deadline**.

<Callout type="danger" title="New preparer pitfall #1">
Virginia is due on <strong>May 1</strong>, not April 15. New preparers assume all state deadlines match federal. Mark this in red.
</Callout>

## Virginia Department of Taxation

The agency is **Virginia Department of Taxation** (tax.virginia.gov), completely separate from the IRS. It has:

- Its own e-file system (Virginia Tax Online Services)
- Its own 760-series forms
- Its own audits
- Its own rates and thresholds

Don't confuse it with **Virginia Employment Commission** (unemployment, 1099-G) or **local Commissioner of the Revenue** (local taxes like business licenses).

## Who must file in Virginia?

Virginia distinguishes three types of taxpayers:

### 1. Resident — <FormRef id="VA-760" />

A person is a **resident** if:

- They are **domiciled** in Virginia (permanent home), OR
- They are a **statutory resident**: maintain a "place of abode" in VA for **more than 183 days** of the year

Domiciliaries pay VA tax on **worldwide income** (all income, anywhere).

### 2. Part-Year Resident — Form 760PY

Moved to/from Virginia during the year. Splits income between VA and non-VA periods.

### 3. Nonresident — Form 763

Lives outside VA but had VA-source income (e.g., remote work with a VA employer, rental in VA). Only taxed on VA-source income.

<Callout type="warning" title="Immigration status ≠ VA tax residency">
A Salvadoran with an ITIN who lives permanently in Annandale IS a Virginia resident for tax purposes, regardless of immigration status. Virginia accepts ITINs and does not ask about immigration status.
</Callout>

## Virginia rates (2024)

Virginia uses a progressive 4-bracket system:

| Taxable Income | Rate |
|---|---|
| $0 – $3,000 | **2%** |
| $3,001 – $5,000 | **3%** + $60 |
| $5,001 – $17,000 | **5%** + $120 |
| $17,001 + | **5.75%** + $720 |

Top rate: **5.75%**. This is moderate: lower than MD (5.75% + local), NY (up to 10.9%), CA (up to 13.3%); higher than no-income-tax states (FL, TX).

## Starting point: federal AGI

Virginia starts from **Federal Adjusted Gross Income (FAGI)** and applies adjustments (additions and subtractions). That means: **if federal is done right, VA is 80% automatic**.

### Common additions (increase VA income)

- Interest on obligations of other states (tax-free federal, taxable VA)
- Lump-sum distributions
- Losses from years when not a VA resident

### Common subtractions (decrease VA income)

- **Age deduction** for 65+
- **Disability income** up to $20,000
- **Social Security benefits** — Virginia **does NOT tax** Social Security (big advantage)
- Military basic pay (limited)
- Virginia 529 contributions

## VA deductions and credits

### VA standard deduction

- $8,500 single (2024)
- $17,000 MFJ

Virginia also allows itemized, but it must match the federal choice (if itemized federally → must itemize in VA).

### Credit for Taxes Paid to Other States

If the taxpayer is a VA resident but paid tax to another state (e.g., works in MD), they get a **credit for the lesser** of what was paid to the other state or what VA would have charged on that income.

<Callout type="info" title="Reciprocity: VA-DC-MD-KY-PA-WV">
Virginia has a **reciprocity agreement** with **DC, Maryland, Kentucky, Pennsylvania, and West Virginia**. This means:
- If you live in VA and work in one of these states, you only pay VA tax on your wages
- The employer in the other state should NOT withhold their state tax
- The employee files **Form VA-4** with the employer (or that state's equivalent to claim exemption)
- If the employer mistakenly withheld, the employee claims refund using the nonresident form of that state

This is **enormously relevant in Northern Virginia**: thousands of Arlington/Alexandria residents work in DC. They only file in VA.
</Callout>

### Virginia EITC

Since 2022 Virginia offers a **refundable 15% of federal EITC** (taxpayer can choose between 15% refundable or 20% nonrefundable). Must qualify for federal EITC first — which **requires a work-valid SSN**. ITIN holders do not qualify.

### Low Income Individuals Credit (CLI)

Nonrefundable credit for taxpayers below the federal poverty line. $300 per claimed exemption.

## Local taxes in Virginia

Virginia has very few local income taxes (unlike MD where each county adds 2-3%). But it does have:

- **Real estate tax** (property, paid to county/city)
- **Personal property tax** (vehicles, each county)
- **BPOL tax** (Business, Professional, Occupational License) if the client has a business

As an income tax preparer, this doesn't directly concern you, but you should know about it to advise business clients.

## Sales tax in Virginia

5.3% base + 0.7% in certain localities (Northern Virginia, Hampton Roads) = **6%** in NOVA. 2.5% on groceries. Does not apply to professional services. As a preparer, if your client has retail, make sure they have a sales tax permit with VA Tax.

## Virginia deadlines

<KeyDate date="May 1" label="Form 760 deadline (NOT April 15)" />

<KeyDate date="November 1" label="Automatic extension up to 6 months" />

- **Extension**: Virginia automatically grants a **6-month extension** without any form, but the estimated payment must be made by May 1 (Form 760IP)
- **Estimated payments**: May 1, June 15, September 15, January 15 (quarters slightly shifted vs federal)

<Callout type="success" title="Best practice">
When you prepare the federal 1040 in March/April, prepare the 760 at the same time but **wait to file until you've verified federal was accepted by the IRS**. This avoids rework if corrections are needed.
</Callout>

## Virginia e-file

Virginia requires e-file if you prepare 50+ returns per year (federal threshold is 10+). Most professional software (Drake, TaxSlayer Pro, ProSeries) includes VA e-file integrated.

## VA penalties

- **Late filing**: 6% of balance due per month, up to 30% max
- **Late payment**: 6% annual interest + 6% penalty
- **Underestimation**: if estimated payments don't reach 90% of actual
- **Fraud**: 100% of underpayment + criminal charges

## VA filing threshold (2024)

Must file if Virginia AGI >:

| Filing status | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Single | $11,950 |
| MFJ | $23,900 |
| Married dependent | $5,950 |

Below these thresholds filing is not mandatory, but it's a good idea if any withholding was taken to recover it as a refund.

## Virginia forms — summary

| Form | Use |
|---|---|
| **760** | Full-year resident |
| **760PY** | Part-year resident |
| **763** | Nonresident (with VA-source income) |
| **763-S** | Nonresident Withholding Refund Claim (reciprocity) |
| **760C** | Underpayment of Estimated Tax |
| **760IP** | Automatic Extension Payment |
| **VA-4** | Employee's Withholding Certificate (W-4 equivalent) |
| **500** | Corporation Income Tax Return |
| **502** | Pass-Through Entity Return |
