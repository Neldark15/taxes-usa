# Setting Up the Business: Legal Structure

Three options to operate your US tax prep business from El Salvador.

## Option A — El Salvador company only

Register a Sociedad Anónima or simply operate as a natural person with NIT in El Salvador.

### Pros

- Simple and familiar
- No US compliance
- No registered agent costs
- Invoice in USD from ES (exported service → potential 13% IVA exemption)
- ES income tax: 30% on profits

### Cons

- **US clients may distrust** paying a foreign company
- International transfers are slower/more expensive
- No "US presence" for marketing
- Some larger clients will ask for W-9 (which you can't properly give without EIN)

### When it makes sense

- Starting out, few clients
- All referred clients (trust already established)
- Not planning to scale soon

## Option B — US LLC ⭐ RECOMMENDED

**Limited Liability Company** single-member (SMLLC) with you as sole non-resident owner.

### Which state to choose

| State | Pros | Cons | Annual cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wyoming (WY)** ⭐ | Cheapest, high privacy, no owner disclosure | — | ~$60 annual report |
| **Delaware (DE)** | Corporate prestige, specialized courts | More expensive, franchise tax $300+ | $300+ |
| **New Mexico (NM)** | Even cheaper, total privacy | Less prestige | $0 annual (requires reseller/agent) |
| **Florida (FL)** | Good reputation, no state tax | $138.75 annual report | $138 |

**Wyoming** is the typical choice for non-residents prioritizing low cost + privacy. Delaware has more "prestige" but is 5x more expensive.

### Do NOT pick Virginia for the LLC

Even though your clients may be in Virginia, **do NOT form the LLC in VA** to start. VA has:

- Annual report fee ($50)
- Requires physical address in VA (your registered agent)
- BPOL tax (local professional license) in some localities

VA only makes sense if you'll have physical presence or employees there.

### Steps to form a Wyoming LLC

1. **Choose a name** (check availability at wyoming.gov/sos)
2. **Registered Agent in WY** (mandatory):
   - Northwest Registered Agent ($125/year)
   - Harbor Compliance ($89/year)
   - Incfile ($119/year)
   - Recommendation: Northwest for mail forwarding service
3. **File Articles of Organization** — online, ~$100
4. **Get EIN from IRS** — critical, see below
5. **Operating Agreement** — private but recommended
6. **Open US bank account**
7. **Beneficial Ownership Information (BOI)** — new FinCEN reporting (currently in legal dispute, verify status in 2025+)

### Getting EIN without SSN

The EIN (Employer Identification Number) is the LLC's tax ID. **Without EIN you can't open a bank account or do business taxes**.

**Problem**: the IRS online form asks for the responsible party's SSN.

**Solution**: <FormRef id="SS-4" /> on paper + fax

1. Complete Form SS-4
2. On line 7b (responsible party SSN), write "Foreign"
3. Send by **fax** to IRS:
   - International Fax: +1 (855) 215-1627 (may change, check on irs.gov)
4. Wait 4-6 weeks (mail also works but slower)
5. Receive CP 575 notice with EIN

<Callout type="success" title="Third-party services">
Services like Doola, Firstbase, StartPack or Wise Business do this whole process (LLC + EIN) for $300-500 in 3-4 weeks. Worth the cost to avoid SS-4 errors.
</Callout>

### Opening a US bank account without traveling

Banks that accept non-resident LLCs online:

- **Mercury** (mercury.com) — fintech, free, very popular for foreign LLCs, requires EIN
- **Relay** (relayfi.com) — similar to Mercury
- **Wise Business** — multi-currency, useful if clients pay in USD
- **Bluevine** — also non-resident friendly

Traditional banks (Chase, BofA, Wells) **almost always require in-person branch visit** and SSN or ITIN. Not viable without travel.

### Form 5472 + 1120 pro-forma — CRITICAL

<Callout type="danger" title="$25,000 penalty">
Every Single-Member LLC with a foreign owner is treated as a "disregarded entity" for income tax, BUT must file <FormRef id="5472" /> every year + a "pro-forma" Form 1120 (with only identification info).

**Penalty for non-filing: $25,000 per year**. This is the biggest and most expensive trap of a non-resident LLC.
</Callout>

- Form 5472 reports transactions between the LLC and the foreign owner (transfers, loans, payments, distributions)
- Filed **together with** Form 1120 on paper (no e-file) to IRS Ogden UT
- Deadline: Apr 15 (with extension via Form 7004 to Oct 15)
- MANDATORY even if the LLC had no income or significant activity

### LLC income tax

- SMLLC with foreign owner = **disregarded entity**
- Income "flows through" to owner as nonresident alien
- **If the service is performed entirely outside USA** (you in El Salvador), generally NO ECI (Effectively Connected Income) → NO federal US income tax
- BUT this area is complex — consult a CPA specialized in non-residents before assuming
- Possible need for personal ITIN if there's US-source income

## Option C — Hybrid (recommended medium-term)

Dual structure:

1. **Wyoming LLC** (commercial arm for US clients):
   - Has EIN, US bank account
   - Invoices services to US clients
   - Revenue lands in the LLC
2. **Company in El Salvador** (actual operation):
   - Where you live and work
   - The LLC pays a "management fee" to the ES company for services rendered
   - Taxes paid in ES on profit

This structure requires bilateral bookkeeping and fair transfer pricing, but is optimal to scale.

## E&O insurance (Errors and Omissions)

**CRITICAL**. Every preparer must have it.

- Covers lawsuits for errors in prepared returns
- Cost: $400-800/year (starting)
- Providers: PICPA, Accountants Professional Liability, Hiscox, 360 Coverage Pros
- **Some providers require US address** — verify before forming the LLC

## Cyber liability insurance

- Covers data breaches (clients have SSN and sensitive documents)
- ~$300-500/year
- MANDATORY under **FTC Safeguards Rule** (applies to all preparers)

## Engagement letter

Contract with each client defining:

- Services included
- Fees and payment terms
- Client responsibilities (provide truthful documents)
- Your liability limitation
- Data privacy
- Jurisdiction (if WY LLC, specify)

Templates available at: AICPA, NATP, your software (TaxDome includes templates).

## GLBA Privacy — Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act

Applies to preparers because they are "financial institutions" per the FTC:

- Must have **Privacy Notice** for clients
- **WISP** (Written Information Security Plan) — see ch. 10
- Data breach notification if it occurs
