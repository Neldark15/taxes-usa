# US Federal Tax System Fundamentals

The US federal tax system is **uniform across all 50 states**: all residents and citizens are taxed under the same federal rules administered by the **Internal Revenue Service (IRS)** and the **Internal Revenue Code (IRC)**. State-level differences only appear in state taxes (covered in the next chapter).

<Callout type="info" title="Key mental model">
**Federal = same for everyone.** Master the federal system and you have 80% of the job done for any client in any state. States vary in rates, forms, and deadlines, but Form 1040 is the same in Virginia, California, or Texas.
</Callout>

## Who must file federal taxes?

The filing requirement depends on three factors:

1. **Tax residency status** (not immigration status)
2. **Filing status** (single, married, etc.)
3. **Gross income** vs. the filing threshold

### Tax residency

For the IRS, a person is a *US tax resident* if any of the following applies:

- **US Citizen** — US citizen (including those born in USA)
- **Green Card holder** (lawful permanent resident) — automatically a resident alien
- **Substantial Presence Test (SPT)** — 31+ days in current year AND formula:
  - Current year days × 1 + Prior year × 1/3 + Two years ago × 1/6 ≥ 183

<Callout type="warning" title="Important for the diaspora">
Immigration status does **not determine** tax status. An undocumented Salvadoran living in Virginia IS a resident alien if they meet SPT and MUST file taxes. A **TPS** holder is almost always a tax resident. A **DACA** holder has an SSN and is a tax resident.
</Callout>

### Filing status

The 5 filing statuses are:

| Status | Who | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Single** | Single, divorced, legally separated | Default |
| **Married Filing Jointly (MFJ)** | Married | Best brackets and deductions |
| **Married Filing Separately (MFS)** | Married filing apart | Loses several credits |
| **Head of Household (HOH)** | Unmarried supporting a dependent | Better brackets than Single |
| **Qualifying Surviving Spouse (QSS)** | Widow/er with dependent child | MFJ rates for 2 years |

## Form 1040: the main return

<FormRef id="1040" /> is the federal individual return. Everything is reported here:

### 1040 Flow (simplified)

```
1. INCOME
   ↓ wages, interest, dividends, business, capital gains, etc.
2. ADJUSTMENTS — Schedule 1
   ↓ IRA, student loan interest, HSA, self-employment deduction
3. AGI (Adjusted Gross Income)
   ↓
4. DEDUCTION (Standard OR Itemized Schedule A)
   ↓
5. TAXABLE INCOME
   ↓ apply brackets
6. CALCULATED TAX
   ↓ subtract credits (CTC, EITC, etc.)
7. TOTAL TAX
   ↓ vs. withholding + payments
8. REFUND or BALANCE DUE
```

### Standard vs. Itemized Deduction

Taxpayer picks the greater of the two:

- **Standard deduction** (annually adjusted, 2024): $14,600 single, $29,200 MFJ, $21,900 HOH
- **Itemized** (<FormRef id="1040" /> Schedule A): mortgage interest, state/local taxes (capped at $10k), medical >7.5% AGI, charity, casualty

Since the Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (2017), the standard is much higher, so **most diaspora taxpayers use standard**.

## Key credits for the diaspora

Credits are more valuable than deductions because they reduce tax **dollar for dollar**.

<Callout type="tip">
**Refundable vs nonrefundable**: a refundable credit gives you money back even if you owe no tax. A nonrefundable one can only bring tax to zero.
</Callout>

### Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC)

- For low/middle-income workers with earned income
- **Requires a work-valid SSN** for taxpayer and spouse (if MFJ)
- **ITIN holders do NOT qualify** for federal EITC
- Up to ~$7,800 with 3+ children (2024)

### Child Tax Credit (CTC) / Additional CTC (ACTC)

- $2,000 per qualifying child under 17
- **Up to $1,700 refundable** via ACTC (2024)
- Child must have a **valid SSN** (ITIN does not work for CTC)
- ITIN holders CAN claim **Credit for Other Dependents** ($500 nonrefundable)

### American Opportunity Credit (AOTC)

- Up to $2,500 per student in the first 4 years of college
- 40% refundable ($1,000 max)
- Requires 1098-T

## Self-Employment Tax

Many diaspora clients work as contractors (construction, cleaning, delivery, Uber). They receive <FormRef id="1099-NEC" /> instead of <FormRef id="W-2" />.

- Reported on **Schedule C** (Profit or Loss from Business)
- **Schedule SE** calculates SE tax: **15.3%** (12.4% SS + 2.9% Medicare) on 92.35% of net income
- 50% of SE tax is deductible as an adjustment (Schedule 1)
- **Quarterly estimated taxes** (Form 1040-ES) required if total tax owed will exceed $1,000

<Callout type="danger" title="Common pitfall">
1099 clients almost always owe taxes in April if they didn't make estimated payments. They can avoid the penalty by meeting the "safe harbor": pay 100% of prior year tax (110% if AGI > $150k) during the year.
</Callout>

<Callout type="info" title="Form 4868 — Extension">
Filing <FormRef id="4868" /> before April 15 extends the **filing time** to October 15, but does **NOT extend payment time**. Unpaid taxes accrue interest and a 0.5%/month penalty if not paid by April 15.
</Callout>

## Withholding and estimated payments

- **W-2 employees**: automatic withholding via Form W-4
- **1099 contractors**: responsible for their own quarterly estimated taxes
- **Refund** = you overpaid (withholding + credits > tax calculated)
- **Balance due** = you underpaid
