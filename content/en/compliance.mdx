# Compliance and Risk Management

What keeps you out of legal trouble. Violations here cost money, credential and reputation.

## Circular 230 — the preparer's bible

Treasury Department Circular 230 is the document regulating tax preparers. Key points:

### Duties (Subpart B)

- Prompt filing of returns
- Use of "best practices"
- No unconscionable fees
- Notify clients of errors
- No omission of required information

### Restrictions (Subpart B)

- Don't negotiate client refund checks
- Don't prepare a return you know contains false info
- Don't charge contingent fees on original returns
- Don't solicit client information by deception

### Sanctions (Subpart D)

- Censure (public reprimand)
- Suspension (up to 5 years)
- Disbarment (permanent)
- Monetary penalties

**You must read Circular 230 at least once completely**. About 80 pages in English, available at irs.gov.

## Form 8867 — Due Diligence for refundable credits

<Callout type="danger" title="$635 penalty per omission (2024)">
When claiming EITC, CTC, ACTC, AOTC, or HOH filing status, you MUST complete Form 8867 for each return. Each omission = **$635 penalty per credit per return**.

A return with poorly-documented CTC + EITC + AOTC = **$1,905 penalty**. For a single client.
</Callout>

### What 8867 requires

1. **Client interview** about eligibility
2. **Document review** supporting eligibility
3. **Knowledge requirement** — facts may be considered reasonable
4. **Documentation retention** — keep 8867 and supports for 3 years

### Required questions

For EITC with qualifying child:

- Did the child live with you more than half the year?
- What documentation do you have (school records, medical, childcare)?
- Can anyone else claim this child?
- Did the child provide more than 50% of own support?

For HOH:

- Are you married? If yes, did you live apart for the last 6 months?
- Did you pay more than 50% of maintaining the home?
- Is there a qualifying person living with you?

### Common supporting documents

- School records (child's address)
- Medical records
- Childcare records
- Social security cards
- Birth certificates
- Lease/utility bills (HOH)

## §6694 — Understatement Penalties

If you prepare a return with tax understatement (client owes more than reported):

- **Negligent**: $1,000 or 50% of fee on the return (greater)
- **Willful**: $5,000 or 75% of fee

### How to protect yourself

- **Reasonable basis**: position has at least 25% chance of being correct
- **Substantial authority**: position has 40%+ chance
- **More likely than not**: position is >50%
- **Disclosure** on Form 8275 if position is "reasonable basis" but controversial

## §6695 — Other Preparer Penalties

Several violations with $60 penalties each (2024):

- **§6695(a)**: Failure to furnish copy of return to taxpayer
- **§6695(b)**: Failure to sign the return
- **§6695(c)**: Failure to furnish PTIN on return
- **§6695(d)**: Failure to retain copy or list
- **§6695(e)**: Failure to file correct information returns
- **§6695(f)**: Negotiating a check from IRS (refund) — $635
- **§6695(g)**: Failure to be diligent with EITC/CTC/AOTC/HOH — $635

These are simple but accumulate. If you do 100 returns and omit PTIN on all = $6,000 penalty.

## E-file mandate

- **11+ returns per year** → must e-file
- Exceptions: client requests paper, return has attachments that don't support e-file, technical problems
- Violation: $50/return up to $25,000 max

## Data Security — IRS Pub 4557 + FTC Safeguards Rule

### Mandatory WISP

**Written Information Security Plan**. Since 2023 mandatory for all preparers.

Must include:

- Designation of a "qualified individual" (can be yourself if solo)
- Annual risk assessment
- Specific safeguards (encryption, access control, monitoring)
- Vendor management
- Incident response plan
- Security awareness training
- Continuous monitoring

Free template: irs.gov/Publication 5708

### Minimum technical safeguards

- 2FA on every system with client data
- Complex passwords (12+ characters)
- Disk encryption (BitLocker/FileVault)
- Network firewall
- Updated antivirus
- VPN for remote connections
- Encrypted offsite backup
- Auto-lock after 15 min inactivity
- HTTPS-only
- Secure deletion of obsolete data

### Data breach response

If you suffer a client data breach:

1. **Contain** the incident (change passwords, isolate systems)
2. **Contact IRS Stakeholder Liaison** — take this seriously
3. **Contact FTC** if applicable
4. **Notify affected clients** (FTC requires within 30 days in many cases)
5. **Contact your E&O insurance** immediately
6. **Consult a lawyer** specialized in data breach

## Knowledge of client fraud

If you discover the client gave you false information:

- **Don't sign the return** if you know there are falsehoods
- **Inform the client** — they have chance to correct
- If client insists on filing with falsehoods: **terminate the relationship**
- Document everything
- Don't unilaterally report to IRS (confidentiality unless law enforcement asks)

## Form 8300 — Cash reporting

If you receive > $10,000 in cash or equivalent (money orders, cashier's checks) from a client:

- Must file Form 8300 with IRS within 15 days
- Also notify the client
- This rule applies to preparers like any business

## Record retention

- **Prepared returns**: 3 years minimum (IRS)
- **8867 and due diligence docs**: 3 years
- **Engagement letters**: 3-7 years
- **WISP and security docs**: indefinitely
- **Some states require more** (NY 6 years, MD 4)

## Preparer Misconduct / IRS OPR

**Office of Professional Responsibility** at IRS handles disciplinary actions against preparers. If a client reports you:

1. OPR investigates
2. If meritorious, sends notice of charges
3. Administrative process (like court but no jury)
4. Penalty can be censure, suspension, disbarment, monetary

**How to avoid this**: follow Circular 230 to the letter, document everything, keep signed engagement letters.
