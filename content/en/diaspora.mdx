# Salvadoran Diaspora Special Cases

This chapter is **the heart of your practice**. Every day you'll see combinations of ITIN, TPS, dependents in El Salvador, FBAR, and remittances. Know the answers before they're asked.

## ITIN — Individual Taxpayer Identification Number

The IRS issues ITINs (format 9XX-XX-XXXX) to people who MUST file taxes but **cannot get an SSN**. Primarily:

- Spouses of US residents without work authorization
- Dependents living in USA (very restrictive)
- TPS/DACA people who lost SSN or request it separately
- **Undocumented** earners of income

### Application process

1. Complete <FormRef id="W-7" />
2. Attach **identity documents**:
   - Valid passport (single best option), OR
   - Two documents from secondary list (birth cert + foreign gov ID, ES driver license, etc.)
3. **File together with Form 1040** (cannot be standalone except specific cases)
4. Send to IRS Austin TX (specific ITIN address)
5. Wait 6-11 weeks

<Callout type="warning" title="Original documents">
If you send originals, IRS returns them — but losses have happened. Safe options:
- **Issuer certification** (El Salvador embassy in DC can certify passports)
- **Certifying Acceptance Agent (CAA)** — certified preparer who makes certified copies
- **TAC Office** — IRS Taxpayer Assistance Center certifies documents
</Callout>

### ITINs expire

- If not used on a return in the last 3 years, it **expires**
- Certain ITIN ranges (middle digits 70-99) already expired in IRS waves
- Renewal with Form W-7 marking "Renewal"

### What an ITIN holder CAN and CANNOT claim

| Item | ITIN holder |
|---|---|
| File 1040 | ✅ Yes |
| Standard deduction | ✅ Yes |
| Itemized deduction | ✅ Yes |
| **EITC (federal)** | ❌ **NO** (requires work-valid SSN) |
| **CTC/ACTC** | ✅ Yes, if child has valid SSN |
| Credit for Other Dependents ($500) | ✅ Yes |
| AOTC (education) | ❌ NO |
| Saver's Credit | ✅ Yes |
| Refund | ✅ Yes (from actual overpayment, not EITC) |

## TPS (Temporary Protected Status)

Salvadoran TPS holders (the country has been under TPS since 2001, redesignated periodically):

- **DO have SSN** (not ITIN, because they have work authorization while TPS lasts)
- Are **resident aliens** for tax purposes if they meet SPT
- Can claim **EITC** (have work-valid SSN)
- Can claim **CTC/ACTC**
- File as any resident alien

<Callout type="success">
TPS clients are the "easy cases" of the diaspora from a tax standpoint — they have SSN and qualify for all credits. The hard cases are undocumented with ITIN.
</Callout>

## DACA (Deferred Action for Childhood Arrivals)

- Have **SSN with restriction** (work-valid while DACA lasts)
- Are resident aliens for tax
- Qualify for full EITC and CTC
- File as standard resident alien

## Dependents living in El Salvador

**Critical rule**: a child living in El Salvador with the mother while the father works in USA **does NOT qualify as a dependent** in most cases.

To claim someone as a **qualifying child**:

- Relationship: child, stepchild, sibling, etc.
- **Age**: under 19 (or under 24 if student)
- **Residency**: **lived with you more than half the year in USA, Canada, or Mexico**
- Support: did not provide more than 50% of own support
- Joint return: does not file joint return

**El Salvador is not on the list** of countries that qualify for the residency test (only USA/Canada/Mexico). That's why dependents in ES almost never qualify as qualifying child.

For **qualifying relative**:

- Dependent's gross income under $5,050 (2024)
- You provided > 50% of support
- Not a qualifying child of anyone else
- Relationship: must be family or household member all year
- **US citizen, national, or resident of US/Canada/Mexico**

<Callout type="danger" title="Watch parents in El Salvador">
Even though the client sends monthly money to parents in El Salvador, they CANNOT claim them as dependents unless parents are US citizens or residents. Remittances do not make anyone a tax dependent. Some unscrupulous preparers do this and create huge audit risk.
</Callout>

## Remittances

- **NOT deductible** for the sender
- **NOT taxable income** for the recipient (it's a gift)
- Not reported on sender's 1040
- Exception: if sending > $18,000/year per person, must report Form 709 (gift tax return), but usually no tax due to $13.6M lifetime exemption

## FBAR — FinCEN 114

**Mandatory** if US tax resident has foreign accounts aggregating > $10,000 USD at ANY point in the year.

### Accounts that count

- Bank accounts (Banco Agrícola, Cuscatlán, Davivienda, Promerica, etc.)
- Investment accounts
- Foreign private pensions
- Joint accounts (counts 100% even if your share is less)
- Signature authority (even if not owner, you can sign on a business account)

### How to file

- **Does NOT go with the 1040**. Filed electronically at **fincen.gov/bsaefiling**
- Deadline: **April 15** with **auto-extension to Oct 15** (no extension request needed)
- No tax — purely informational
- But **penalties are severe**:
  - Non-willful: up to $10,000 per violation (can be per account, per year)
  - Willful: up to $100,000 or 50% of balance, per year

<Callout type="danger" title="FBAR is the biggest trap">
Many Salvadoran clients keep savings accounts in ES "for emergencies" or "for parents". Always ask. The penalty for unfiled FBAR is larger than 10 years of fees.
</Callout>

## FATCA — Form 8938

Similar to FBAR but different:

- Filed **with the 1040** (not FinCEN)
- Much higher threshold: $50k single or $100k MFJ living in US
- Higher thresholds for Americans living abroad
- Includes more asset types than FBAR (PFICs, foreign mutual funds)
- **Can duplicate with FBAR** — file BOTH if applicable

## Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (Form 2555)

- Only applies if taxpayer **lives outside USA** — i.e., for Americans abroad
- **Does NOT apply to the diaspora** living in USA
- Excludes up to $126,500 (2024) of foreign earned income if Physical Presence Test (330 days) or Bona Fide Residence Test is met

## Foreign Tax Credit (Form 1116)

- For when a US resident pays tax to El Salvador on the same income
- Prevents double taxation
- Typical case: client sells property in ES and pays Salvadoran tax, then must report the gain in USA

## ITIN vs SSN decision matrix

```
Valid work SSN?
├── Yes (US citizen, Green Card, TPS, DACA, work visa)
│   └── Full resident alien → all credits
└── No
    ├── Lives in USA with intent to stay?
    │   └── Resident alien via SPT → full taxation
    │       └── Uses ITIN → NO EITC, YES CTC if child has SSN
    └── Temporary visitor?
        └── Nonresident alien → 1040-NR, different rules
```
